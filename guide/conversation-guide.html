Conversation guide for managers

Understanding what drives results

A light structure for the conversation that turns your team's results into shared understanding, and then into one agreed step.

Your role in the conversation

You are a facilitator and listener. As a manager, you set the tone, encourage a safe environment and foster healthy, respectful team conversations.

Reminder for managers

Be curious

Treat the result as a signal to understand, not a verdict to defend.

Listen before deciding

Hear examples and context before moving to solutions.

Share ownership

Involve the team in what matters and in what happens next.

Start with the overall team results

Open the discussion by looking at the team's overall results. Use whichever visual supports the conversation best: this guide, a one pager, a slide deck or another results view. The aim is to create a shared picture before discussing the focus areas.

Overall results and focus areas

Present the overall results first, then show the three generated focus areas. Check what resonates with the team and guide them to select one focus area to explore more deeply. Do not try to cover all three in one meeting.

Further guidance on each agenda point

Use these prompts as a light reference. Adapt them to the flow of the conversation rather than reading them as a script.

1

Open the conversation

Set the purpose: understand the experience behind the results before deciding what to do. Create safety by agreeing to listen to understand, make room for different experiences and discuss patterns rather than individual responses.

Conversation starter

"These results tell us where to look, not why the results are what they are. I would like us to understand the experience behind them together."

2

Look at the overall results and select a focus area

Open the discussion with the overall team results. You can use this guide, a one pager, a slide deck or another visual that fits the context. Present the three generated focus areas, check whether they resonate with the team, and guide the team to select one area to explore further.

Open the picture

What do you recognise in this picture?

Check resonance

What feels incomplete or surprising?

Which of the three focus areas feels most important or useful for us to explore?

Choose one focus area together. The manager guides the selection, but should not decide in isolation.

3

Take a moment to celebrate

If a celebration point is available, use it to notice what is going well and make the team's contribution visible. Share a few concrete examples of behaviours you see day to day, without turning the moment into a speech.

Notice

What is helping this work well?

Build

When have you experienced this at its best?

What should we protect, repeat or build on?

4

Dive deeper into the selected focus area and set concrete agreements

Use the generic conversation starters below for whichever focus area the team selected. Choose two or three, listen to the responses, ask a follow up when useful, check whether others recognise the pattern and summarise what you hear.

Keep listening

  • "Can you say a little more?"
  • "What else is important here?"
  • "I'm hearing … Did I capture that accurately?"

Use the deeper question only if the team wants to go further.
