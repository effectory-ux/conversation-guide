1

Open the conversation

Set the purpose: understand the experience behind the results before deciding what to do. Agree to listen, to understand, make room for different experiences, and discuss patterns rather than individual responses.

Say something like

“These results tell us where to look, not why they are what they are. I would like us to understand the experience behind them together.”

2

Look at the results as a whole

Open the picture: What do you recognize? What stands out?

Check resonance: What feels incomplete or surprising? Which focus area should we explore together?

3

Take a moment to celebrate

Notice: What is helping this work well? When have you experienced this at its best?

Build: What should we protect, repeat or build on?

4

Dive deeper, then agree on a next step

For your chosen focus area, pick two or three questions below. Listen, ask follow-up questions, then summarize what you hear.

Notice: What stands out to you about this result?

Example: What does this look like in our day to day work? Can you think of a recent example?

Context: What was happening when the survey was running?

Driver: What seems to be making this easier or harder?

Difference: Does anyone experience this differently? What might explain the difference?

Influence: What part of this is within our team’s influence, and what needs support elsewhere?

Meaningful change: What would make a meaningful difference?

Keep the conversation going

“Can you say a little more?” · “What else is important here?” · “I am hearing … Did I capture that accurately?”

What you agree together

Decide whether to improve, monitor or need support, then set one small step and name who owns it.

5

Close the meeting

Wrap up the conversation

“Today we heard … We agreed to … The first step is … [name] will … by …”

“What is the most important thing we should take forward?”

Thank everyone, and make sure the agreement and the follow up moment are visible to the team afterwards.
